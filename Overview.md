In correspondence to my information in my log...I will say, however frustrating all of this may have been, it did remind me of a reading on Slow Data and Datafication in Archaeology. What I gathered from this reading was that it can be beneficial to slow down and try to understand the data and machines we are working with in archaeology before rushing to get the final product or big data as they say. And that is exactly what I found myself doing this week, was rushing to find the big data. With these activities, it feels as though the data is the most important thing. And the actual history, which is arguable the more interesting aspect, gets lost in the data which removes the enchanting quality from it, as seen in Perry's article. 
In class, what we were discussing this week, practical necromancy, as Dr. Graham likes to say, is actually very interesting to me. I find it interesting to discover problematic patterns in the machines that we use for archaeology such as AI. But I do wish the work was not so reliant on the tech, because that black box removes archaeologists from the connection to what they are working on in a way. It feels bitter sweet. The tech is so helpful, but also very blinding.
